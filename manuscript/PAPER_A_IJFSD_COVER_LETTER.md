**Re: Submission — "Toward nutrition-sensitive digital food trust in peri-urban Nigeria: A Home Economics framework and GroChain case study in Ilorin Metropolis, Kwara State"**

We are pleased to submit this manuscript for consideration in the *International Journal on Food System Dynamics*. The paper contributes to your journal's mission of analysing the organisation, management, operation, and strategic development of food systems and their actors—through a multidisciplinary lens combining food-system policy, consumer trust, digital innovation, and household-level food access.

**Why IJFSD is the appropriate outlet**

This manuscript is not a narrow agtech technical report. It examines how **integrated digital trust infrastructure**—traceability, marketplace access, logistics, financial inclusion, and offline USSD channels—may strengthen nutrition-sensitive food-system dynamics in a peri-urban Nigerian setting. IJFSD's explicit welcome of **case studies** and research on food chain actors, institutions, and policy aligns directly with our structured analysis of GroChain within the Ilorin Metropolis food environment.

**Contribution and novelty**

1. We propose a **Home Economics–grounded conceptual framework** mapping digital food trust components onto FAO food security pillars—a perspective underrepresented in digital agriculture literature.  
2. We synthesise Nigeria-focused digital agriculture evidence (2018–2026) and identify a gap: few platforms integrate traceability, fintech, and last-mile USSD access while targeting nutrition-sensitive outcomes.  
3. We present **verified secondary contextual data** for Kwara State and Ilorin Metropolis alongside an **implementation case study** of GroChain, distinguishing deployed features from roadmap aspirations with academic transparency.  
4. We derive **actionable policy implications** for Kwara State, Home Economics extension, and national digital agriculture strategy—without claiming unverified empirical nutrition outcomes.

**Methodological note**

This paper uses **narrative review and structured case study methodology** based on published literature, national survey statistics, and open-source platform documentation. **No primary human subjects data were collected.** We believe this approach is appropriate for IJFSD's case study section and provides a foundation for future empirical evaluation.
