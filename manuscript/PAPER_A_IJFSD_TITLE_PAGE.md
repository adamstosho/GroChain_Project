## Abstract (150–250 words recommended for IJFSD)

Nigeria's food system faces fragmentation, post-harvest losses, financial exclusion, and weak consumer trust in agricultural produce—conditions that undermine household food access despite local production capacity. Digital agriculture platforms have expanded rapidly, yet most evaluations emphasise yield or technology adoption rather than nutrition-sensitive food-system outcomes. This paper develops a conceptual framework linking integrated digital food trust infrastructure to the availability, access, utilisation, and stability dimensions of food security, with explicit grounding in Home Economics household resource theory. Using narrative review methodology and structured case study analysis, we synthesise literature on digital agriculture in Nigeria (2018–2026), present secondary contextual evidence for Ilorin Metropolis and Kwara State—including 41% child stunting and substantial reliance on urban agriculture for household income—and describe GroChain, a Nigeria-focused platform integrating QR traceability, digital marketplace commerce, shipment logistics with spoilage-risk alerting, BVN-linked financial inclusion, USSD access, and partner-mediated cooperative onboarding. We distinguish verified platform capabilities from aspirational roadmap features and map GroChain modules onto nutrition-sensitive food-system pathways. Policy implications are drawn for Kwara State extension services, Home Economics curricula, and Nigeria's National Digital Agriculture Strategy. We conclude that bundled, offline-capable digital trust platforms represent a promising but under-evaluated class of food-system intervention requiring rigorous mixed-methods outcome research in peri-urban West Africa.
